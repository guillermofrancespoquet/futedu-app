# FUTEDU – Nutrition Kickoff Session (60 min)
### Interactive quiz-dialogue format · English

**Goals of this session:**
1. Gauge the group's current nutrition knowledge (basic–intermediate, no jargon)
2. Set the body composition standard for the year (bioimpedance target: **< 12.5% body fat**)
3. Let them vote on the recipes we'll cook together in upcoming workshops

**Format:** You call out each question like a Kahoot round ("Hands up for A, B, C, or D!" / "Shout it out!"). After each answer, spend 30–60 seconds turning it into a short back-and-forth — ask *why* they picked that answer, correct gently, connect it to their training. Don't just move on to the next question; let 2–3 kids explain their reasoning before you reveal the right one.

---

## 0. Welcome (5 min)

> "Hey everyone! Today's not a lecture — it's a quiz. I want to see what you already know about food and your bodies, then I'll tell you what we're aiming for this year, and you'll get to pick the recipes we cook together. Ready?"

Quick round: ask each person their name + one food they eat every single day. Light, fast, breaks the ice.

---

## 1. Nutrition Knowledge Check (25 min)

Run these like rapid-fire rounds — multiple choice, hands up, then discuss. 15 questions, ~100 seconds each. Each one has a single, unambiguous correct answer — if a kid argues a reasonable alternative, use it as a talking point, but the table answer is the one to confirm.

| # | Question | Options | Answer & talking point |
|---|----------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | Which macronutrient is your body's primary and fastest fuel source during high-intensity exercise (like a sprint)? | A) Protein B) Carbohydrates C) Fat D) Vitamins | **B.** Glycogen from carbs = quick-access fuel. Ask: "What should be on your plate a few hours before a match?" |
| 2 | Which nutrient is most directly responsible for repairing and building muscle tissue after training? | A) Carbohydrates B) Protein C) Fiber D) Sugar | **B.** Bridges to the post-workout recovery snack later. |
| 3 | True or False: waiting until you feel thirsty is the best way to know when to drink water during training. | True / False | **False.** Thirst kicks in after you're already a bit dehydrated — sip regularly instead. |
| 4 | Roughly how much water should an active teen aim for on a normal training day (including food)? | A) 0.5 L B) 1 L C) 2–2.5 L D) 5 L | **C** (rough guide — more on hot/high-sweat days). Ask who actually tracks this. |
| 5 | Which breakfast would best fuel a morning training session? | A) Just black coffee B) Oatmeal with fruit and an egg C) A candy bar only D) Skipping breakfast | **B.** Balanced carbs + protein beats empty sugar or nothing at all. |
| 6 | True or False: skipping meals is an effective way to get leaner. | True / False | **False.** It usually backfires on energy, performance, and recovery. |
| 7 | Which food group is your main source of fiber? | A) Fried food B) Vegetables, fruit, and whole grains C) Candy D) Soda | **B.** Fiber = digestion + feeling full. |
| 8 | What's the main role of dietary fat in your body? | A) It's useless and should be avoided B) Hormone production and long-term energy storage C) It's only found in junk food D) It only makes you gain weight | **B.** Dispels the "all fat is bad" myth. |
| 9 | Isotonic sports drinks are designed mainly to replace what during long, sweaty sessions? | A) Vitamins B) Water and electrolytes (like sodium) C) Protein D) Fiber | **B.** Direct bridge to the recipe vote later. |
| 10 | True or False: sugar from a piece of fruit affects your body exactly the same way as sugar from candy. | True / False | **False.** Fruit comes packaged with fiber, water, and vitamins that change how it's digested — candy doesn't. |
| 11 | Which mineral, often low in teenage diets, is especially linked to fatigue if you don't get enough? | A) Iron B) Sodium C) Vitamin C D) Fiber | **A.** Common gap, especially relevant for training teens. |
| 12 | What's a smart window for eating a recovery snack (protein + carbs) after an intense session? | A) Within a couple of hours B) Only the next morning C) It doesn't matter at all D) Never eat after training | **A.** General guidance — reinforces the post-workout recipe. |
| 13 | Before training, which snack is generally the smarter choice? | A) A fast-digesting carb like a banana B) A heavy, high-fat meal C) Both are exactly the same D) Skip food entirely | **A.** Digests faster, less stomach discomfort during exercise. |
| 14 | What does "body composition" actually measure? | A) Just your total body weight B) The ratio of fat mass to lean mass (muscle, bone, water) C) Your height D) How fast you run | **B.** Direct segue into Part 2. |
| 15 | True or False: two people can weigh exactly the same but have very different body compositions. | True / False | **True.** Perfect lead-in to bioimpedance. |

**Wrap this section:** *"Nice work — you actually know more than you think. Now let's talk about what we're going to measure and aim for this year."*

---

## 2. This Year's Body Composition Standard (15 min)

Keep this conversational, not clinical.

**Talking points to hit, with built-in check-ins:**

- *"Who's had a bioimpedance scan before? What did it tell you?"* — let a couple answer.
- Explain simply: bioimpedance estimates how much of your body is fat vs. lean mass (muscle, bone, water) by sending a tiny painless electrical signal through you.
- **This year's target: body fat below 12.5%.** Frame it as a *team performance standard*, not a weight-loss goal — lower body fat in this range (for a training athlete) generally means more usable muscle relative to fat, which supports speed, power, and recovery.
- Important nuance to say out loud: *"This is a guide, not a race to the bottom — going too low isn't healthy either, and everyone's starting point is different. We'll measure, track, and adjust individually."*
- Ask: *"What's one habit you think could move your number in the right direction — sleep, snacks, water, or something else?"* Let 3–4 kids answer; affirm whatever's reasonable.
- Mention that measurements will happen periodically through the year so they see their own progress.

**Quick check for understanding (mini quiz, 2 questions):**

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|----------|--------|
| 16 | True or False: Body composition tells you more than the scale alone. | **True** |
| 17 | This year's team target for body fat is below what %? | **12.5%** |

---

## 3. Vote on the Workshop Recipes (10 min)

> "Over the next sessions, you'll actually cook these yourselves. Today you vote on what we make first."

Present each option with one energetic line, then a show-of-hands vote. Keep a tally on a whiteboard/phone.

1. **Homemade granola** — "Your own snack, no hidden sugar, ready in one workshop."
2. **Make-your-own pre-workout snack** — "Something quick and easy you can prep yourself before training."
3. **Make-your-own post-workout recovery snack** — "The recovery bite that actually helps your muscles bounce back."
4. **Homemade isotonic drink** — "The hydration drink from Question 8 — but made by you, from real ingredients."

After the vote:
- Announce the winning 1–2 recipes for the next workshop.
- Ask: *"Why did you vote for that one? What do you already put in something similar?"* — 2 minutes of open discussion, gather any dietary restrictions/allergies casually here (useful for you to note before the cooking workshop).

---

## 4. Wrap-up (5 min)

- Recap in one sentence: *"Today: we checked what you know, set our target — under 12.5% body fat — and you picked what we're cooking next."*
- Tell them when/where the first recipe workshop and their first bioimpedance measurement will happen.
- Quick final show-of-hands: *"Who's excited to cook next session?"* — end on energy.

---

### Facilitator notes
- Total run time: 60 min (5 + 25 + 15 + 10 + 5). If a group runs slower, trim to 10–11 quiz questions in Part 1 rather than cutting Part 2 or 3.
- Keep score if it helps energy (first correct answer per question gets a point), but don't let it slow the dialogue down — the goal is discussion, not speed.
- Note who mentions allergies/dietary restrictions during the recipe vote for your workshop planning.
