# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Frozen Yogurt Bark: A Snack Ready for Tomorrow

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 3 tables of 5–10 players (up to 30), one tray per table
**Recipe covered:** *Frozen Yogurt Bark*
**Session length:** 60 min

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Each table makes **one tray** (about 10 pieces) with unsweetened soy yogurt, banana, berries, oats, seeds and a little dark chocolate. No oven, no bowls, no blender: the banana is mashed into the yogurt **in the tray** with a fork.
- The trays need **3 hours in the freezer**, so the bark is ready the same evening or the next day. The facilitator breaks the frozen bark into pieces afterwards and portions it into containers (or the players do it when they collect it); eat it straight from the freezer.
- Each table **decides its own toppings** within the ingredient list (more berries, no chocolate, extra seeds...): the trays come out different and it gives the group something to talk about.
- The activity (**Yogurt Lineup**) is a short, hands-on label-reading exercise. Context for the facilitator is in Section 4.

### Run of show (60 min)

| Min | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Hook and a guess.** Hold up two tubs (a plain soy and a coconut or oat one, covering the labels) and ask: "Which one has more protein?" Show of hands. Keep the answer for the end. | Facilitator |
| 5–10 | **Demo of the bark:** line the tray, mash the banana into the yogurt, spread 1 cm thick, scatter toppings, press in. | Facilitator |
| 10–25 | **Each table makes its tray** (15 min): decide toppings, make the bark. | Tables |
| 25–28 | Trays into the freezer (flat; make room beforehand). | Facilitator + 1 per table |
| 28–32 | **Explain the lineup** (4 min): show where the numbers are on a real tub (see Section 4). | Facilitator |
| 32–42 | **Yogurt Lineup** (10 min): each table fills in the sheet from the four tubs. | Tables |
| 42–50 | **Share-back and reveal** (8 min): answers, then the guess from the start. | Tables |
| 50–55 | Clean-up. | Everyone |
| 55–60 | **Wrap-up line** and when the bark will be ready. | Facilitator |

**Before the players arrive (~20 min):** buy and label four real tubs (A–D, see Section 3); set out for each table the tray, cling film, a fork, a chopping board and knife, the yogurt and toppings; clear the freezer shelves; print one Yogurt Lineup sheet per table; write down the label numbers of the four tubs yourself so you can check the tables' answers quickly.

---

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

A frozen yogurt bark is a **snack that fits the recovery window**: yogurt brings protein (and calcium when it is fortified), banana and berries bring carbs and natural sweetness, oats and seeds bring fibre, healthy fats and crunch. It feels like a treat without relying on added sugar.

**Framing for the group:** a **snack made today and eaten tomorrow**, ideal after training. Compare it with the energy balls (Session 3, before training: quick carbs) and the brownie (Session 4, a treat): this one is about **protein plus carbs, after**. It is a light snack, not a meal: two pieces are about 150 kcal.

**Talking point:** what is a "plant-based yogurt"? It leads straight into the activity: **not all plant-based yogurts have the same protein**. Soy yogurt is usually the highest of the plant options; coconut or oat yogurts are often much lower.

**Honest notes:** the nutrition depends heavily on the yogurt chosen, so always check the label. Bark is a snack, not a protein source on its own.

## 3. Shopping List — 30 people max (3 trays)

One tray (~20 × 30 cm) makes about 10 pieces, so 3 trays give about **30 pieces: one piece per player** (a full portion is 2 pieces). If you want 2 pieces each, use bigger trays or add a fourth one (and 500 g more yogurt).

| Ingredient | Per tray | Total (3 trays) | Buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsweetened soy yogurt | 500 g | 1.5 kg | 3 × 500 g (or 1.5 kg) |
| Ripe bananas | 1 | 3 | 4 (1 spare) |
| Mixed berries (fresh or frozen, thawed) | ⅔ cup (100 g) | 300 g | 1 bag (500 g) |
| Rolled oats | 3 tbsp (30 g) | 90 g | pantry |
| Pumpkin seeds, chopped | 2 tbsp (20 g) | 60 g | 1 small bag |
| Dark chocolate (vegan, 70 % or more), chopped | 20 g | 60 g | 1 bar (100 g) |
| Maple syrup (optional) | 1 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 1 small bottle |

**Combined totals to buy:** 1.5 kg unsweetened soy yogurt · 4 bananas · 500 g berries · seeds · 1 dark chocolate bar · maple syrup (optional) · oats (pantry).

**For the activity — 4 real yogurt tubs (about 125–150 g each), labelled A–D:**
- **A:** plain unsweetened soy yogurt (the one you use for the bark).
- **B:** a sweetened or flavoured soy yogurt or dessert.
- **C:** a coconut or oat yogurt (plant-based, usually lower in protein).
- **D (optional reference):** a plain dairy yogurt (Greek-style if you like).
Choose them in the shop by reading the labels: the point is that they differ.

**Supplies:** 3 trays (~20 × 30 cm) that fit the freezer · cling film or baking paper to line them · 3 forks or spoons · 3 chopping boards and knives · 30 disposable containers with lids for the pieces · freezer space for 3 trays lying flat · 3 printed **Yogurt Lineup** sheets · pens.

**Allergy note:** contains **soy** and **oats** (check for gluten). Seeds and chocolate may carry traces of nuts: read the labels. A dairy-free chocolate is needed for a vegan result.

## 4. Activity — Yogurt Lineup (about 10 min + share-back)

### The context: why this activity
Players hear "plant-based" and assume "healthy" or "high in protein". It is not necessarily true: some plant yogurts (soy) have protein close to dairy, others (coconut, oat) have very little, and flavoured ones can carry as much sugar as a dessert. The skill we want them to leave with is simple: **read the nutrition table (per 100 g) and the first three ingredients before choosing**, and do one small calculation with it.

### What the players actually do
1. They get four real tubs (A–D) and one sheet per table.
2. For each tub they copy **four things** from the label into the table: the type of yogurt, **protein per 100 g**, **sugars per 100 g**, and the **first three ingredients**.
3. They answer four questions: which has the most and the least protein; which has the most sugar and whether any is added (is there sugar or syrup in the ingredients?); which tub they would put on the bark and why; and how many grams of their tub give 10 g of protein (a division).
4. At the end they hear the answer to the guess from the start of the session.

### Step by step
| Min | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Take tub A and show on it: (1) the **nutrition table**, always read the column "per 100 g"; (2) the **ingredients list**, the first ones weigh the most; (3) the difference between "sugars" and **added sugar** (look for sugar, syrup, fruit puree). Do the sum once on the board with the worked example on the sheet. |
| 4–14 | Tables fill in the table (the four tubs travel from table to table) and answer the questions. Circulate: help with reading, not with the answers. |
| 14–22 | **Share-back:** one table per question. Ask "who disagrees?" and check against your own notes of the labels. Reveal the guess from minute 0. |

### Example of a filled sheet (invented numbers, only to show what it can look like)
| Tub | Type | Protein per 100 g | Sugars per 100 g | First 3 ingredients |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Soy, plain | 4.5 g | 0.5 g | soy base, ferments, calcium |
| B | Soy, flavoured | 3.5 g | 11 g | soy base, **sugar**, fruit puree |
| C | Coconut | 1.0 g | 4 g | coconut base, starch, ferments |
| D | Dairy, plain | 4.5 g | 4 g | milk, ferments |

Questions: most protein = A (and D); least = C. Most sugar = B, and the sugar is added (it is listed second). On the bark = A. 10 g of protein from A = 10 ÷ 4.5 × 100 ≈ 220 g. **Use the real numbers from your own four tubs, not these.**

**Facilitator key:**

| Question | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| Most and least protein | Soy is usually the highest of the plant options; coconut or oat the lowest. The numbers vary by brand: check yours. |
| Most sugar, and added? | Flavoured or sweetened tubs have far more sugar. If sugar, syrup or fruit puree is listed near the top, part of it is added. |
| Which on the bark | A high-protein, low-sugar tub; the sweetness comes from the banana and the berries. |
| 10 g of protein | Grams = 10 ÷ (protein per 100 g) × 100. A tub with 4 g per 100 g needs 250 g. It shows why a bark piece is a snack, not a protein hit. |

**Wrap-up line:** *"'Plant-based' does not tell you the protein or the sugar. The label does: compare per 100 g and read the first three ingredients."*
