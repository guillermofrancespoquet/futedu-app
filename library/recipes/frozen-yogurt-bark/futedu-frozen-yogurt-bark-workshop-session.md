# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Frozen Yogurt Bark: A Snack Ready for Tomorrow

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 3 tables of 5–10 players (up to 30), one tray per table
**Recipe covered:** *Frozen Yogurt Bark*

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Each table makes **one tray** (about 10 pieces) with unsweetened soy yogurt, banana, berries, oats, seeds and a little dark chocolate. No oven, no bowls, no blender: mash the banana into the yogurt **in the tray** with a fork.
- Flow (~15 min): line the tray → mash banana into yogurt and spread it 1 cm thick → scatter the toppings and press them in → tray into the freezer.
- It needs **3 hours in the freezer**, so it is ready the same evening or the next day. Pieces are broken up and stored in a container in the freezer; eat straight from the freezer.
- Let each table **decide its toppings** within the ingredient list (more berries, no chocolate, extra seeds...). It makes the trays different and gives material for the activity.
- *Extra activity:* **Yogurt Lineup** (~10 min), see Section 4. It runs naturally while the trays are still being topped, or right after.

---

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

A frozen yogurt bark is a **snack that fits the recovery window**: yogurt brings protein (and calcium when it is fortified), banana and berries bring carbs and natural sweetness, oats and seeds bring fibre, healthy fats and crunch. It is a way to eat a snack that *feels* like an ice cream or a treat without relying on added sugar.

**Framing for the group:** a **snack made today and eaten tomorrow**, ideal after training. Compare it with the energy balls (Session 3, before training: quick carbs) and the brownie (Session 4, a treat): this one is about **protein plus carbs, after**. It is also a light snack, not a full meal: two pieces are about 150 kcal, so it does not replace dinner.

**Talking point:** ask what a "plant-based yogurt" even is. This leads straight into the activity: **not all plant-based yogurts are equal in protein**. Soy yogurt is usually the highest of the plant options; coconut or oat yogurts are often much lower in protein.

**Honest notes:** values depend heavily on the yogurt chosen, so always check the label. Frozen bark is a snack, not a protein source on its own; pair it with a proper meal.

## 3. Shopping List — 30 people max (3 trays)

One tray (~20 × 30 cm) makes about 10 pieces, so 3 trays give about **30 pieces: one piece per player** (a full portion is 2 pieces). If you want 2 pieces each, use bigger trays or add a fourth one (and 500 g more yogurt).

| Ingredient | Per tray | Total (3 trays) | Buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsweetened soy yogurt | 500 g | 1.5 kg | 3 × 500 g (or 1.5 kg) |
| Ripe bananas | 1 | 3 | 4 (1 spare) |
| Mixed berries (fresh or frozen, thawed) | ⅔ cup (100 g) | 300 g | 1 bag (500 g) |
| Rolled oats | 3 tbsp (30 g) | 90 g | pantry |
| Pumpkin seeds, chopped | 2 tbsp (20 g) | 60 g | 1 small bag |
| Dark chocolate (vegan, 70 % or more), chopped | 20 g | 60 g | 1 bar (100 g) |
| Maple syrup (optional) | 1 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 1 small bottle |

**Combined totals to buy:** 1.5 kg unsweetened soy yogurt · 4 bananas · 500 g berries · seeds · 1 dark chocolate bar · maple syrup (optional) · oats (pantry).

**For the activity:** 4 real yogurt tubs (about 125–150 g each) to compare, for example: unsweetened soy · sweetened or flavoured soy · coconut or oat · (optional) plain dairy yogurt as a reference. Check labels in the shop: the point is that they differ.

**Supplies:** 3 trays (~20 × 30 cm) that fit the freezer · cling film or baking paper to line them (so the bark lifts out) · 3 forks or spoons · 3 chopping boards and knives (for the chocolate and fruit) · containers for the pieces (30 disposable containers with lids) · freezer space for 3 trays lying flat · 3 printed **Yogurt Lineup** sheets · pens.

**Allergy note:** contains **soy** and **oats** (check for gluten). Seeds and chocolate may carry traces of nuts: read the labels. A dairy-free chocolate is needed for a vegan result.

## 4. Activity — Yogurt Lineup (~10 min)

**Goal:** read a real label and see that **"plant-based" does not tell you the protein or the sugar**.

**Materials:** printed **`futedu-frozen-yogurt-bark-yogurt-lineup.pdf`**, one per table, pens, and the four real yogurt tubs, labelled A–D, passed around the tables.

**How it runs (~10 min):**
1. Each table fills in the table from the labels, using the numbers **per 100 g**: type, protein, sugars and the first three ingredients.
2. They answer the four questions on the sheet, including "how many grams of your tub give you 10 g of protein?".
3. Share-back (3 min): which tub went on the bark and why.

**Facilitator key:**

| Question | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| Most and least protein | Usually soy is the highest of the plant options (and dairy Greek-style yogurts higher still, if you included one); coconut or oat yogurts are often the lowest. Always **use the actual labels**, the numbers vary by brand. |
| Most sugar, and added? | Flavoured or sweetened tubs have far more sugar. Look for sugar, syrup or fruit puree early in the ingredients: that is added sugar. |
| Which on the bark | A high-protein, low-sugar tub: the sweetness comes from the banana and berries. |
| 10 g of protein | Grams = 10 ÷ (protein per 100 g) × 100. For example, a tub with 4 g per 100 g needs 250 g. This shows why a bark piece is a snack, not a protein hit. |

**Wrap-up line:** *"'Plant-based' does not tell you the protein or the sugar. The label does: compare per 100 g and read the first three ingredients."*
