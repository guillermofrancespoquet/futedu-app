# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Dips: One Starter, Three Ways (no blender)

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 3 tables of 5–10 players (up to 30), one dip per table, shared as a starter
**Recipes covered:** *Guacamole*, *White Bean & Lemon Dip* and *Vegan Sobrasada* (all without a blender)
**Session length:** 60 min

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Each table makes **one dip**. At the end every dip goes on a **shared plate** in the middle of the tables and everyone tastes all three with vegetable sticks and pita, like a starter. The quantities are for sharing (about a tablespoon of each dip per person), not for a full portion.
- **No blender.** The guacamole and the white bean dip are made with a **fork or potato masher** in a bowl. The **vegan sobrasada** is the rustic, no-blender version: the sweet potato is cooked in the **microwave** and mashed with a fork; the **garlic and the sun-dried tomatoes are chopped very finely** and mixed in with the paprika, the oregano, the salt and the **extra virgin olive oil**; the mushrooms are sautéed by the facilitator (with the table helping a little).
- The sweet potato and the mushrooms are the only things that need heat, so the facilitator **starts the sobrasada table first**: sweet potato into the microwave and mushrooms into the pan right after the demo, while the other tables begin.
- Players tasted dips last year (yogurt sauce, hummus and vegan sobrasada): ask what they remember, then explain that today's three are different dips, except the sobrasada.
- The activity (**Dip Judge**) happens at the tasting.

### Run of show (60 min)

| Min | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Hook:** "If you only had crisps, or a dip with vegetables, which keeps you full until dinner, and why?" Mention last year's dips. | Facilitator |
| 5–10 | **Assign dips and demo.** One minute on each dip: what goes in and the one rule ("mash with the fork, don't chase a smooth texture"). Start the sobrasada table first: sweet potato diced into the microwave (6–8 min), mushrooms into the pan (5–7 min). | Facilitator |
| 10–25 | **Make the dips (15 min).** Two players per table also cut the dippers (carrots, cucumber, pepper, pita triangles). | Tables |
| 25–30 | **Shared plates:** each table puts its dip on a plate in the centre with a spoon; dippers on the side. | Tables |
| 30–42 | **Tasting and Dip Judge** (12 min): each table scores the three dips and builds its snack plate. | Tables |
| 42–50 | **Share-back** (8 min): which dip fills you up the most, and which you would not eat right before training. | Tables |
| 50–55 | Clean-up. | Everyone |
| 55–60 | **Wrap-up line.** | Facilitator |

**Before the players arrive (~25 min):** wash and prepare the vegetables whole (the players cut them); dice the sweet potato and put it in a microwave-safe dish with a lid and a splash of water; chop the mushrooms; set the pan on the stove; set out for each table the bowl or container, a fork or masher, a chopping board and knife, and the ingredients for its dip; print three Dip Judge scorecards.

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

Dips are a simple way to turn **vegetables and wholemeal bread** into something people want to eat. The three dips cover different nutrients: **guacamole** is rich in unsaturated fats and fibre (that is why it keeps you full), the **white bean dip** brings plant protein and fibre, and the **vegan sobrasada** is a savoury, smoky spread made from sweet potato, mushrooms and sun-dried tomatoes, with paprika for the colour and the flavour of the traditional pork sobrasada.

**Framing for the group:** a **starter or an afternoon snack**, not something to eat right before training: the fat and fibre digest slowly. The lesson is that **the dip makes it filling and the dippers make it nutritious**: a dip with only bread is a very different snack from a dip with carrots, cucumber and pepper.

**Talking point:** "which keeps you full until dinner: crisps or dip with vegetables?"

**Honest notes:** dips are energy-dense (olive oil, avocado), so the portion matters. A tablespoon or two is a starter; a whole bowl is a meal.

**Allergy note:** avocado and legumes can be allergens; ask the group. Wholemeal pita contains **gluten**: offer rice cakes or extra vegetables as an alternative. The vegan sobrasada has no common allergens in this recipe (check the label of the sun-dried tomatoes, which can contain sulphites).

## 4. Activity — Dip Judge (12 min tasting + 8 min share-back)

**Goal:** make players think about **why a dip is filling**, and about **when** they would use it.

**Materials:** printed **`futedu-dips-judge-card.pdf`**, one per table (three in total), pens, and the three dips on shared plates.

**How it runs:**
1. Each table tastes the three dips and scores each from **1 to 5** for **taste** and **texture**.
2. They tick what makes each one filling (**fat, protein, fibre**) and when they would eat it (before training, snack, dinner).
3. They build a **snack plate** on the sheet: one dip, one vegetable, one carb and one extra.
4. **Share-back:** which dip fills you up the most and which you would not eat right before training; one table per question.

**Facilitator key:**

| Question | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| What makes it filling | Guacamole: unsaturated fat and fibre. White bean dip: plant protein and fibre (the olive oil adds fat). Sobrasada: sweet potato carbs and fibre, plus the olive oil. |
| Before training? | Most dips are not ideal right before training: the fat and fibre digest slowly. Better as an afternoon snack or with dinner. |
| Snack plate | A dip, a vegetable for volume and colour, a carb (pita) for energy, and an extra with protein (beans, hummus, yogurt sauce) if the dip has little. |

**Wrap-up line:** *"A good dip is a vehicle: fat, protein and fibre make it filling, and the dippers decide what you get. Vegetables and a carb turn it into a snack."*
