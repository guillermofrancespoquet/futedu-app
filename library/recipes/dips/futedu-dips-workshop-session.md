# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Dips: One Snack, Three Ways (no blender)

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 3 tables of 5–10 players (up to 30), one dip per table
**Recipes covered:** *Guacamole*, *White Bean & Lemon Dip* and *Vegan Sobrasada* (recipe to be added)

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Each table makes **one dip** for the whole group, then every table **passes its dip around** so that everyone tastes all three with vegetable sticks and pita.
- Equipment: **no blender**. The guacamole and the white bean dip are made with a **fork or potato masher** in a bowl (or a large disposable container). The vegan sobrasada is pending: its method will be added when the recipe is confirmed, and if it needs a blender we will adapt it (for example, a finely chopped version, or one prepared at home and brought to the session).
- Flow: tables make their dip (~15 min) → everyone cuts vegetable sticks and pita triangles (~10 min) → tasting round with the scorecard (~10 min).
- A dip is a **vehicle**: the dippers (vegetables, wholemeal pita) decide what the snack gives you. Let every player build their own plate.
- Players tasted dips last year (yogurt sauce, hummus and vegan sobrasada): ask what they remember and what they liked, then explain that today's dips are different from those.
- *Extra activity:* **Dip Judge** (~10 min), see Section 4.

---

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

Dips are a simple way to turn **vegetables and wholegrain bread** into something people actually want to eat. The three dips cover different nutrients: **guacamole** is rich in unsaturated fats and fibre (that is why it keeps you full), the **white bean dip** brings plant protein and fibre, and the **vegan sobrasada** is a savoury spread (details when the recipe is added).

**Framing for the group:** a **snack for the afternoon or a light dinner starter**, not something to eat right before training: the fat and fibre digest slowly. The lesson is that **the dip makes it filling, and the dippers make it nutritious**: a dip with only bread is a very different snack from a dip with carrots, cucumber and pepper.

**Talking point:** ask "if I only had crisps or dip with vegetables, which keeps you full until dinner and why?"

**Honest notes:** dips are energy-dense (olive oil, avocado): the portion matters. Two or three tablespoons per person is a snack; a whole bowl is a meal.

## 4. Activity — Dip Judge (~10 min)

**Goal:** make players think about **why a dip is filling**, and about **when** they would use it.

**Materials:** printed **`futedu-dips-judge-card.pdf`**, one per table, pens, and the three dips on the tables.

**How it runs (~10 min):**
1. Each table tastes the three dips and scores each from 1 to 5 for **taste** and **texture**.
2. They tick what makes each one filling (**fat, protein, fibre**) and when they would eat it (before training, snack, dinner).
3. They build a **snack plate**: one dip, one vegetable, one carb and one extra.
4. Share-back (3 min): which dip fills you up the most and which you would not eat right before training.

**Facilitator key:**

| Question | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| What makes it filling | Guacamole: unsaturated fat and fibre. White bean dip: plant protein and fibre (olive oil adds fat). Sobrasada: depends on the recipe. |
| Before training? | Most dips are not ideal right before training: the fat and fibre digest slowly. Better as an afternoon snack or with dinner. |
| Snack plate | A good plate has a dip, a vegetable for volume and colour, a carb (pita) for energy, and an extra with protein (beans, hummus, yogurt sauce) if the dip has little. |

**Wrap-up line:** *"A good dip is a vehicle: fat, protein and fibre make it filling, and the dippers decide what you get. Vegetables and a carb turn it into a snack."*
