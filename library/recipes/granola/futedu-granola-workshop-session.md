# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Granola: Batch Snack, Energy

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** working in teams of 2–5 (adjust based on turnout)
**Recipe covered:** *Crunchy Granola with Almonds, Walnuts & Raisins*

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Split into teams of 2–5. Each team preps and bakes their own tray, following the recipe steps together (mix → coat → bake → cool → add raisins).
- Since granola needs to cool completely to go crunchy, plan for a short gap between the bake finishing and the tasting/activity — this is a good moment to run the activity below while the trays cool.
- *Extra activity:* **Scenario Cards — "Does granola fit here?"** (~10-12 min) — see Section 3.

---

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

Oats provide slow-release carbs, and the almonds, walnuts and pumpkin seeds layer on healthy fats and protein — which is exactly why granola isn't a one-size-fits-all snack. It's energy-dense and digests more slowly than something like the overnight oats toppings, so *when* and *how much* matters more than with a lot of other snacks. Framing it as a **portioned batch snack** (not something to eat straight from the bag) is the main habit to reinforce this session — it's a great option to have ready for the week, but the serving size and timing decide whether it helps or just adds empty extra calories.

---

## 3. Activity — Scenario Cards: "Does granola fit here?" (~10-12 min)

**Goal:** move past "granola is healthy" into actually thinking about when and how to use it — the session's real focus.

**Materials to prepare beforehand:** one scenario card per team (or per pair of teams if the group is small), each describing a moment of the day. Suggested scenarios:

1. **Breakfast before school, no time to cook**
2. **Afternoon snack, ~2 hours before training**
3. **Right after a match, not very hungry yet**
4. **Late at night, just before bed**
5. **Long bus trip to an away game**
6. **Between two training sessions on the same day**

**How it runs:**
1. Hand each team one scenario (with more than 6 teams, repeat scenarios — different teams landing on different answers for the same one makes for a better debate).
2. Give them 3–4 minutes to discuss: *Does granola fit well here? Why or why not? What would you pair it with — yogurt, milk, fruit, or on its own?*
3. Each team presents their scenario and their call to the group. Open it up briefly for other teams to agree or challenge before you weigh in.

**Facilitator answer key (for your reference — don't hand this out):**

| Scenario | Good fit? | Why / how to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast before school | Yes | Quick and easy — pair with milk or yogurt for extra protein to start the day. |
| Afternoon snack, ~2h before training | Yes, with care | Fine as a snack, but keep the portion controlled — the fat content digests slowly, so eating a big handful right before training isn't ideal. |
| Right after a match | Not ideal as the main recovery food | The fat slows digestion when the body wants fast carbs + protein — better to lean on something like the post-workout snack first, with granola later as a topper if still hungry. |
| Late at night before bed | Not the best choice | Energy-dense and slow to digest — a small amount is fine, but it's not the ideal bedtime snack. |
| Long bus trip to an away game | Yes | Portable, no fridge needed, easy to self-portion — one of its best use cases. |
| Between two sessions same day | Situational | Works better paired with something faster-digesting (fruit) alongside it, rather than as the only recovery food between sessions. |

**Wrap-up line for the group:** *"Granola isn't good or bad — it's about matching the ingredient to the moment. Same food, different answer depending on when you eat it and how much."* This connects directly back to the pre-workout framing from the overnight oats session.
