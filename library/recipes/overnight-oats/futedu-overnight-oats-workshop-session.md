# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Overnight Oats: Pre-Workout Breakfast (2 variations)

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 10 participants · working in teams of 2–5 (adjust based on turnout)
**Recipes covered:** *Overnight Oats with Blackberries, Banana & Walnuts* and *Overnight Oats with Banana, Blueberry, Almonds & Peanut Butter*

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Split the group into teams of 2–5 depending on how many show up. Each team makes **one jar of each recipe** (so everyone tastes both by the end).
- Walk the group through the steps together first (mix → refrigerate → topping), then let each team assemble their own jars at their station.
- Since overnight oats need to rest, the jars won't be ready to eat the same session — either prep a few jars in advance so everyone gets to taste the texture/result today, or plan a quick tasting the next morning.
- *Extra activity:* **Ingredient-Benefit Relay** (~15 min) — see Section 4.

---

## 2. What These Recipes Bring & How They Help Performance

**Shared base (both recipes):** oats + banana provide a steady release of carbohydrates — the main fuel for training. Chia seeds add fiber and plant-based omega-3, and help the mix thicken overnight. Framing both as a **pre-workout breakfast**: something the players can prep the night before an early session, so they're not skipping breakfast or grabbing something low-quality on the way in.

**Recipe 1 — Blackberries, Banana & Walnuts:**
Walnuts add plant-based omega-3s, which support recovery and have an anti-inflammatory role useful for players training frequently. Blackberries bring fiber and antioxidants, helping counter the oxidative stress that comes with intense exercise. The touch of honey tops up quick-access carbs right before a session.

**Recipe 2 — Banana, Blueberry, Almonds & Peanut Butter:**
Almonds and peanut butter add healthy fats and extra protein, which help keep players fuller for longer — useful on days with a gap between breakfast and training. Blueberries contribute antioxidants similarly to the blackberries in Recipe 1.

**Talking point for the group:** both recipes are built the same way — swap the toppings and you can make endless versions. This is a good moment to get them thinking about their own combinations for future sessions.

## 4. Activity — Ingredient-Benefit Relay (~15 min)

**Goal:** get the group to actually connect each ingredient to what it does, in a fast, physical format instead of a lecture.

**Materials to prepare beforehand:**
- 9 **ingredient cards** (one per card, large text, can include a small icon/drawing): Rolled oats · Chia seeds · Banana · Walnuts · Blackberries · Honey · Blueberries · Almonds · Peanut butter
- 9 **benefit cards**, matching 1-to-1 with the ingredients above:
  1. Rolled oats → **Slow-release carbs / lasting energy**
  2. Chia seeds → **Fiber & plant-based omega-3**
  3. Banana → **Fast carbs / potassium**
  4. Walnuts → **Plant-based omega-3 / recovery**
  5. Blackberries → **Antioxidants & fiber**
  6. Honey → **Quick energy boost**
  7. Blueberries → **Antioxidants**
  8. Almonds → **Healthy fats & protein**
  9. Peanut butter → **Protein & healthy fats / satiety**
- A stopwatch or phone timer, tape or cones to mark a start line, a table at the far end for the benefit cards.

**Set-up:**
- Spread the 9 benefit cards face-up on the table at the "finish" end of the kitchen/room.
- Stack the 9 ingredient cards face-down at the start line.
- Split into the same teams of 2–5 used for cooking.

**How it runs:**
1. On "go," the first player from each team runs to the start line, grabs the top ingredient card, runs to the table, and places it next to the benefit card they think matches.
2. They run back and tag the next teammate, who repeats with the next ingredient card.
3. Keep going until all 9 ingredient cards are placed. Stop the clock when the last card is down.
4. Fastest team wins the heat — but matches aren't checked until the debrief, so speed alone doesn't guarantee the win (see scoring below).

**Running it with 2–5 teams:** if there's room for more than one start line/table, run heats in parallel and just compare final times. Otherwise run teams one at a time as a "beat the clock" leaderboard — keeps energy up and gives everyone a turn to watch and start predicting matches before their go.

**Scoring:** fastest time wins only if all matches are correct. Add **+5 seconds per wrong match** to a team's final time — this way rushing without thinking actually costs them, not just going slow.

**Debrief (a few minutes):** go through the 9 pairs one by one. For each, ask a player from any team *why* they think that match makes sense before you confirm it — reinforces the reasoning instead of just handing them the answer.

**Optional tie-breaker / filler if a team finishes very fast:** a couple of rapid true/false call-outs, e.g. *"Chia seeds have basically no fiber"* (false) or *"Honey gives you slower energy than oats"* (false) — answer by pointing to one side of the room or the other.
