# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Rice Cake Bar: Six Breakfasts and Snacks, One Base

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 3 tables of 5–10 players (up to 30)
**Recipe covered:** *Rice Cake Bar*: a rice cake with six topping combinations

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Each table gets a **full bar**: rice cakes, the six toppings, plates and a spoon or knife per topping. No cooking, no oven, no bowls.
- Each player builds **two rice cakes**: one for **before training** and one for **after training**, and says why. They can taste other combinations by swapping a bite with a neighbour.
- The six toppings are chosen to cover different moments: **sweet energy** (peanut butter + banana), **savoury** (avocado + tomato + lemon), **protein** (soy yogurt + berries + seeds), **plant protein** (hummus + cucumber), **quick sweet** (tahini + date) and the **classic** (tomato + olive oil + salt).
- Prep before the players arrive (~15 min): slice the bananas, cucumber and dates and open the tubs. Leave the avocado for the last minute (it browns). Rice cakes go soft quickly once topped, so everyone assembles and eats right away.
- *Extra activity:* the **Bar Menu** sheet (~15 min), see Section 4.

---

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

A plain rice cake is **quick carbs with very little protein or fibre**: light and easy to digest, which makes it a handy base, but on its own it would leave you hungry soon. So the **topping does the work**: peanut butter and banana add protein, fat and more carbs; avocado adds healthy fat; yogurt and hummus add protein; dates give quick sugars; tomato with olive oil is the light classic.

**Framing for the group:** one base, many different breakfasts or snacks. The session teaches that a "meal" is a **combination** (carb + something with protein or fat + colour) and that the right combination depends on **the moment**: a light, easy one before training, a protein one after, a savoury one for dinner. It is the breakfast/snack version of the timing idea from the previous sessions.

**Talking point:** ask "which of these would you pick 45 minutes before a match, and which would you pick on the bus home?" Let them argue.

**Honest notes:** rice cakes are not special: bread, toast, corn cakes or a tortilla work too. They are used here because they need no preparation and show how much the topping changes the result. Not every combination is for every moment (avocado is rich in fat, so it digests slowly: a good dinner or breakfast, not the last thing before kick-off).

**Combined totals to buy:** 6 packs rice cakes · 1 jar peanut butter · 12 bananas · 6–7 avocados · 8 tomatoes · 3 lemons · 1 kg soy yogurt · 500 g berries · seeds · 400 g hummus · 2 cucumbers · 1 small jar tahini · 12 dates · olive oil and salt (pantry).

**Supplies:** 3 cutting boards and knives · 3 sets of spoons or butter knives (one per topping, to avoid cross-contact) · paper plates (60) · napkins · 3 printed **Bar Menu** sheets · pens.

**Allergy note:** the bar contains **peanuts**, **soy** (yogurt), **sesame** (tahini and often hummus). Run it so that every topping has its own spoon, and keep the peanut butter at its own end of the table. Most rice cakes are gluten-free but check the pack if someone has coeliac disease.

## 4. Activity — Bar Menu: Build Your Two (~15 min)

**Goal:** get players to choose a combination **for a moment**, not just for taste.

**Materials:** printed **`futedu-rice-cake-bar-topping-menu.pdf`**, one per table, and pens. The sheet lists the six toppings (what goes on, what it brings, when it fits) and has a small table for "my two cakes".

**How it runs (~15 min):**
1. Explain the menu in two minutes and let them build.
2. Each player writes down the cake they built for **before training** and for **after training**, with one sentence on why.
3. Share-back (4 min): ask 3 or 4 players to defend one choice. Use the two questions at the bottom of the sheet: "which one would still leave you hungry, and what would you add?" and "which would you avoid right before training?".

**Facilitator key:**

| Question | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| Before training (1–1.5 h) | Peanut butter + banana, tahini + date, or tomato + olive oil. Easy to digest, quick carbs. Avocado or lots of seeds slow digestion: better kept for another moment. |
| After training | Soy yogurt + berries + seeds, or hummus + cucumber (protein), ideally with extra carbs on the side. |
| Still hungry? | Plain rice cake plus berries only, or tomato only: add peanut butter, hummus or yogurt for protein. |
| Avoid right before | Avocado-heavy or seed-heavy cakes: slower to digest. |

**Wrap-up line:** *"There is no perfect breakfast: there is the right one for the moment. The same rice cake changes completely with what you put on it."*
