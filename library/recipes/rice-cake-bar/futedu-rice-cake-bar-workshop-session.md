# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## Rice Cake Bar: Six Breakfasts and Snacks, One Base

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** 3 tables of 5–10 players (up to 30), each table split into **2 groups** (6 groups of about 5)
**Recipe covered:** *Rice Cake Bar*: a rice cake with six topping combinations
**Session length:** 60 min

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Each table gets a **full bar**: rice cakes, the six toppings, plates and a spoon or knife per topping. No cooking, no oven, no bowls. The two groups of a table share its bar.
- To keep the explanations short, the **design work is done by groups of about 5** and one **spokesperson per group** presents (1 minute each: six groups, about 9 minutes in total). Then everyone builds and eats their own rice cakes freely.
- The six toppings cover different moments: **sweet energy** (peanut butter + banana), **savoury** (avocado + tomato + lemon), **protein** (soy yogurt + berries + seeds), **plant protein** (hummus + cucumber), **quick sweet** (tahini + date) and the **classic** (tomato + olive oil + salt).
- Rice cakes go soft after about 15 minutes once topped, so everything is assembled and eaten right away.

### Run of show (60 min)

| Min | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Hook:** "You have 20 minutes between school and training. What do you eat?" Show a plain rice cake and ask what is missing from it (protein, fibre, flavour). | Facilitator |
| 5–10 | **Demo and groups.** Build one cake with peanut butter and banana to show how fast it is; read the six toppings from the menu. Split each table into two groups and give out the roles: **2 builders, 1 writer, 1 spokesperson, 1 taster**. One sheet per group. | Facilitator |
| 10–25 | **Group design (15 min):** taste the toppings in small bites, choose **one cake for before training and one for after training**, fill in the sheet ("Our pair": the combos and one sentence of why), build the two cakes on a plate. | Groups |
| 25–35 | **Presentations (about 9 min):** each spokesperson shows the two cakes in 1 minute; the facilitator asks one question and adds one comment from the key. Applause, no voting. | Spokespersons |
| 35–50 | **Free build and eat (15 min):** everyone makes and tastes their own cakes (suggest at least one new topping they have not tried). | Everyone |
| 50–55 | Clean-up, leftovers into containers. | Everyone |
| 55–60 | **Wrap-up line.** Ask for one new combination they will try at home. | Facilitator |

**Before the players arrive (~20 min):** slice the bananas, cucumbers and dates; put the toppings in separate dishes with their own spoon on each table; open the tubs; set out the plates. Leave the avocado for the last minute (it browns). Print **6 sheets** (one per group) and have pens ready.

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

A plain rice cake is **quick carbs with very little protein or fibre**: light and easy to digest, which makes it a handy base, but on its own it would leave you hungry soon. So the **topping does the work**: peanut butter and banana add protein, fat and more carbs; avocado adds healthy fat; yogurt and hummus add protein; dates give quick sugars; tomato with olive oil is the light classic.

**Framing for the group:** one base, many different breakfasts or snacks. A "meal" is a **combination** (carb + something with protein or fat + colour) and the right combination depends on **the moment**: a light, easy one before training, a protein one after, a savoury one for dinner.

**Talking point:** ask "which of these would you pick 45 minutes before a match, and which would you pick on the bus home?" Let them argue.

**Honest notes:** rice cakes are not special: bread, toast, corn cakes or a tortilla work too. They are used here because they need no preparation and show how much the topping changes the result. Not every combination is for every moment (avocado is rich in fat, so it digests slowly: a good dinner or breakfast, not the last thing before kick-off).

## 4. Activity — Topping Menu: Design Your Pair (25 min, groups of about 5)

**Goal:** get players to choose a combination **for a moment**, not just for taste, and to say why in one minute.

**Materials:** **6 printed copies of `futedu-rice-cake-bar-topping-menu.pdf`** (one per group) and pens. The sheet lists the six toppings (what goes on, what it brings, when it fits), a box for **"Our pair"** (one cake for before training, one for after, with one sentence of why) and the roles.

**How it runs:**
1. Each group reads the menu, tastes the toppings in small bites and agrees on **two cakes**: one for 1 to 1.5 h before training and one for within 1 h after.
2. The writer fills in the sheet; the builders make the two cakes on a plate; the taster gives an honest verdict.
3. **Presentations:** each spokesperson shows the plate: what is on each cake, what it brings, why it fits the moment (1 minute), and answers one question.
4. Use the two questions on the sheet when you add comments: "which one would still leave you hungry, and what would you add?" and "which would you avoid right before training?".

**Facilitator key:**

| Question | What to listen for |
|---|---|
| Before training (1–1.5 h) | Peanut butter + banana, tahini + date, or tomato + olive oil. Easy to digest, quick carbs. Avocado or lots of seeds slow digestion: better for another moment. |
| After training | Soy yogurt + berries + seeds, or hummus + cucumber (protein), ideally with extra carbs on the side. |
| Still hungry? | A plain rice cake with only berries, or only tomato: add peanut butter, hummus or yogurt for protein. |
| Avoid right before | Avocado-heavy or seed-heavy cakes: slower to digest. |

**Wrap-up line:** *"There is no perfect breakfast: there is the right one for the moment. The same rice cake changes completely with what you put on it."*
