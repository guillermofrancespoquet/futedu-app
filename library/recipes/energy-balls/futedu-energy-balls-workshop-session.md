# FUTEDU Nutrition Workshop — Session Master Document
## No-Bake Peanut Butter & Cocoa Energy Balls: Pre-Training Fuel

**Location:** Residencia Galileo Galilei — kitchen
**Group:** working in teams of 5–6 (adjust based on turnout)
**Recipe covered:** *No-Bake Peanut Butter & Cocoa Energy Balls*

---

## 1. Session Dynamic

- Split into teams of 5–6. Each team mixes and rolls **one full batch** (12 balls) together, following the steps as a group (mash dates → mix → roll → chill).
- No oven, no cooking — the only wait is the fridge chill (~30 min), so this is a good moment to run a short activity while the balls firm up.
- Everyone leaves with their own portion (~2 balls) to take home, and extras are shared out for tasting during the session.
- *Extra activity:* **Energy Ball Challenge** (~10 min) — see Section 4.

---

## 2. What This Recipe Brings & How It Helps Performance

Dates are the base of this recipe — mostly natural sugars, which give a **fast** energy hit. Rolled oats sit alongside them and digest more slowly, so the combination avoids the sharp spike-and-crash you'd get from dates alone. Peanut butter and chia seeds add a bit of protein and healthy fat, which helps the snack hold players over rather than leaving them hungry again in 20 minutes.

**Framing for the group:** this is a **pre-training snack**, eaten roughly 1–1.5h before a session — close enough to training that the fast carbs from the dates are put to use quickly, not stored. It's the opposite framing from the granola session (a slower-digesting, portion-controlled batch snack) — a good contrast to draw for the players: same "batch-prepped snack" idea, different timing rules.

**Talking point:** ask the group when they'd actually eat these versus the granola from the previous session — reinforces that timing changes what's the "right" choice, not just the ingredient list.

## 3. Shopping List — ~33 people, ~2 balls per person (6 teams, 1 batch each)

Base recipe (1 batch = 12 balls) is scaled **×6** below, one batch per team.

| Ingredient | Per batch (×1) | Total (×6 teams) | Buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitted dates, packed | 1 cup (~150 g) | 6 cups (~900 g) | 2 bags (~500 g each) |
| Rolled oats | 1 cup (~90 g) | 6 cups (~540 g) | 1 bag (1 kg) |
| Peanut butter (or almond butter) | ½ cup (~130 g) | 3 cups (~780 g) | 2 jars (~400 g each) |
| Cocoa powder | 2 tbsp (~12 g) | ¾ cup (~72 g) | 1 small tin/bag |
| Chia seeds | 1 tbsp (~10 g) | 6 tbsp (~60 g) | 1 small bag |
| Salt | 1 pinch | 6 pinches | pantry staple |

**Combined totals to buy:** ~900 g pitted dates · 1 kg rolled oats · ~780 g peanut butter · ~72 g cocoa powder · ~60 g chia seeds · salt (pantry).

**Supplies:** 1 large mixing bowl per team (6), a fork or potato masher for the dates, small containers or bags for take-home portions (1 per person), fridge space for 6 trays/plates while chilling.

**Swap station (for Section 4 activity):** 3 small bowls to set out — dried cranberries or raisins, ground cinnamon, and shredded coconut. That's all that's needed; no extra buying beyond a small bag of each.

**Allergy note:** peanut butter is the main allergen here — have an almond-butter or sunflower-seed-butter swap ready for anyone with a peanut allergy, and confirm before the session.

## 4. Activity — Energy Ball Challenge (~10 min)

**Goal:** get teams to apply the nutrition logic behind the recipe (fast vs. slow carbs, protein/fat for satiety) to their own ingredient swap, instead of just following the steps mechanically. Trimmed down to a quick, low-prep version — no handouts, no writing, just a stir and a spoken pitch.

**Materials to prepare beforehand:**
- The **swap station** only: 3 small bowls — dried cranberries or raisins, ground cinnamon, shredded coconut. That's it, nothing else to hand out.

**How it runs:**
1. After rolling their base batch (recipe step 3), each team sets aside **3-4 balls' worth of mix** and picks **one** of the three swap-station items to stir in or roll the portion in — the rest of the batch stays as the standard recipe for take-home.
2. While everything chills, the team agrees out loud on **one sentence**: what they changed and whether it's still good pre-training fuel — no writing, no cards.
3. Once trays come out of the fridge, each team gives a **15-second spoken pitch** to a neighboring team and offers a taste. Facilitator gives quick, informal feedback on the spot — not graded, just light coaching.

**Facilitator answer key (for your reference — don't hand this out):**

| Swap | Effect on the nutrition logic |
|---|---|
| Dried cranberries/raisins instead of some dates | Still fast-carb dominant, slightly less sweet, adds a bit more fiber. Fine as pre-training fuel. |
| Cinnamon | Flavor only — no meaningful nutrition shift. Good "safe" pick for a team that wants to keep it simple. |
| Rolled in coconut | Adds a little extra fat — barely changes the balance in a small coating amount, still fine close to training. |

**Wrap-up line for the group:** *"Almost any swap here still works — the real skill is knowing WHY it still works, not just that it tastes good. That's the difference between guessing and actually understanding your nutrition."*
